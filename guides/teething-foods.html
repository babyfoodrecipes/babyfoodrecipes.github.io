Teething Foods

Teething usually starts at around six months, just as babies begin eating solids, and sore gums can make eating uncomfortable. Cold, firm foods that a baby can gnaw on can bring real relief, and they double as finger foods that build chewing skills. But not every teething food is safe: hard foods can break off and become choking hazards, and sweetened teething biscuits are bad for emerging teeth. This guide covers the safest teething foods, how to offer them, and what to avoid.

How teething affects eating

Teething can make a baby's gums sore, swollen, and tender, which may reduce their appetite for a few days. Some babies drool more, chew on everything, and become fussy at mealtimes. Others eat normally throughout. Refusing food during teething is common and usually short-lived: keep offering milk and soft foods, and do not worry if intake drops for a day or two. If your baby refuses all food and milk for more than a day, or has a high fever, talk to your doctor, because teething alone does not cause high fever.

Safe teething foods

Cold foods work best for sore gums. Try chilled cucumber sticks with the seeds removed, cold melon slices, or a frozen banana in a mesh feeder. Steamed carrot sticks cooled in the fridge are firm enough to gnaw but soft enough to be safe. A cold, wet flannel for chewing is a simple option. Toast fingers, especially lightly toasted bread crusts, give a baby something firm to bite. A silicone teether chilled in the fridge is a good non-food option. Always supervise your baby with any teething food, and sit them upright.

Foods to avoid while teething

Avoid hard foods that can snap off in chunks, such as raw carrot sticks, hard apple slices, and hard rusks, because the pieces can block the airway. Biscuits marketed for teething are often high in sugar and can damage new teeth, so use them rarely if at all. Do not give frozen food directly from the freezer in large pieces, as it can be too hard and too cold. Avoid teething gels that contain numbing agents unless your doctor recommends them, and never give a baby a bottle of juice or sweetened drink to soothe them.

Other ways to soothe sore gums

Food is only one way to help. A clean finger gently rubbed on the gums can bring relief, as can a chilled teething ring. Distraction also helps: play, a walk, or a favourite toy can take your baby's mind off the discomfort. If your baby seems in pain, infant paracetamol or ibuprofen can help, but follow the dosing instructions and ask your pharmacist or doctor if you are unsure. Teething is a normal stage, and most babies cope well with a mix of cold foods, comfort, and patience.

Coping with tough teething days

Some teething days are harder than others, and it helps to have a plan. Keep a few chilled teethers in the fridge, a bag of frozen banana pieces for a mesh feeder, and a clean, wet flannel ready to offer. Offer soft, cool foods such as yoghurt, cucumber sticks, and melon slices, and avoid hard or crunchy foods that hurt sore gums. Give your baby extra cuddles and comfort, because teething can make them clingy and unsettled. If your baby is in pain, infant paracetamol or ibuprofen can help, but follow the instructions carefully. Most teething phases pass within a few days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can teething cause a fever?

Teething can cause a slightly raised temperature, but not a high fever. A temperature above 38 degrees Celsius is not caused by teething alone, so seek medical advice.

Are teething biscuits safe?

Most are safe if they dissolve easily, but many are high in sugar. Cold vegetables, toast fingers, and chilled teethers are healthier choices.

Should I offer cold food to a teething baby?

Yes. Cold cucumber, melon, or a chilled teether can soothe sore gums. Avoid foods that are frozen solid, as they can be too hard.

Important: every baby is different. Always follow your pediatrician's advice on when to start solids, which foods to offer, and how to introduce allergens. The guidance on this site is general information, not medical advice.
