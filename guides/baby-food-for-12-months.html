Baby Food for 12 Months

At twelve months, your baby becomes a toddler, and food changes in several ways at once. They can now drink whole cow's milk as their main milk, eat most family foods chopped small, and join fully in family meals. They also need less milk than before, because food now provides more of their nutrition. This guide explains the changes that happen at twelve months, which new foods become suitable, and how to manage the move to family eating.

Moving to whole milk

From twelve months, whole cow's milk can replace formula or breast milk as the main drink, if you choose. Offer about 350 millilitres a day, usually in a cup rather than a bottle, since prolonged bottle use can affect teeth. Do not give skimmed or semi-skimmed milk as the main drink before age two, because toddlers need the fat in whole milk for growth. Milk should be a drink alongside meals, not a replacement for them. If your baby is still breastfeeding, you can continue for as long as you both wish.

Eating family meals

At twelve months, your baby can eat most of what the family eats, as long as it is cut to a safe size and not too salty. Pasta, rice, potatoes, bread, meat, fish, eggs, beans, and most vegetables are all suitable. Cook family meals without salt and add seasoning to adult portions at the table. Let your toddler eat with the family at the table, which teaches table manners and encourages them to copy what others eat. Expect mess: toddlers are learning, and food on the floor is normal.

New foods at twelve months

Honey is now safe, although there is no need to add it to food. Your toddler can try whole milk, and can eat foods that were previously avoided, such as soft cheese made from unpasteurised milk if it is well cooked. They can also manage harder textures, such as raw apple slices and raw carrot sticks, as long as you supervise them. Whole nuts are still a choking hazard and should wait until age five, but smooth nut butters are fine. Keep offering a wide variety so your toddler keeps trying new foods.

How much should a one-year-old eat?

Appetite varies enormously at this age. A typical day is three meals and two snacks, with milk alongside. Some days your toddler will eat a lot, other days almost nothing, and that is normal. Offer regular meals and snacks, let your toddler decide how much to eat, and avoid pressure or bribes. Keep portions small and let them ask for more. If you are worried about growth or appetite, talk to your pediatrician rather than comparing your child with others.

The first birthday and beyond

The first birthday is a good moment to look at how far your baby has come, from first spoonfuls to family meals. After twelve months, food becomes more important than milk, and your toddler will eat three meals and two snacks a day. Keep offering a wide variety of foods, including ones your toddler refused before, because tastes change and it can take ten or more tries before a child accepts a new food. Keep mealtimes relaxed and regular, let your toddler decide how much to eat, and enjoy watching them become an independent eater. If you have worries, your pediatrician can help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my one-year-old have honey now?

Yes. Honey is safe from twelve months, as the risk of infant botulism has passed. There is still no need to add it to food.

How much milk should a one-year-old drink?

About 350 millilitres of whole milk a day is enough. More than that can reduce appetite for solid food and lower iron intake.

Should I stop breastfeeding at twelve months?

No, you do not have to. You can continue breastfeeding for as long as you and your child wish, alongside family foods.

Important: every baby is different. Always follow your pediatrician's advice on when to start solids, which foods to offer, and how to introduce allergens. The guidance on this site is general information, not medical advice.
