Baby Food for 9 Months

At nine months, your baby is becoming a confident eater. They can probably pick up finger foods, manage lumps and thicker textures, and eat three meals a day alongside milk feeds. This is a good age to move away from smooth purees and towards the chopped, mashed family foods they will eat as toddlers. This guide explains what nine-month-old babies typically eat, which textures to offer, and how a day of meals might look.

What changes at nine months

By nine months, most babies have better hand control and can pick up small pieces of food with their thumb and finger. They can chew soft lumps, even without teeth, and they are more interested in feeding themselves. Milk is still important, but solids now provide a bigger share of their nutrition. Many babies drop a milk feed at this stage, although they still need breast milk or formula alongside meals. Appetite varies widely: some nine-month-olds eat large amounts, others eat very little, and both can be normal.

Textures to offer

Move from smooth purees to mashed and chopped foods with soft lumps. Offer pasta pieces, soft-cooked vegetables, small pieces of meat, and mashed beans. Finger foods become more important: toast fingers, strips of omelette, steamed vegetable sticks, and soft fruit pieces all help your baby practise chewing and self-feeding. Avoid foods that are still smooth and runny, because your baby needs practice with texture. If your baby gags on lumps, go back a step for a few days and try again; gagging is common while they learn.

A sample day at nine months

A typical day might start with porridge with mashed banana, followed by a milk feed. Lunch could be lentil and vegetable soup with a bread finger and a few pieces of soft pear. An afternoon snack might be yoghurt with berries. Dinner could be chicken and sweet potato mash with steamed broccoli, followed by a milk feed before bed. Offer water in a cup with meals. This is only an example: the exact foods matter less than the pattern of three meals, one or two snacks, and regular milk feeds.

New foods to try

Nine months is a good time to broaden your baby's diet. Try fish, well-cooked shellfish, and a wider range of vegetables such as aubergine, mushrooms, and sweetcorn. Offer mild herbs and spices, and let your baby try small amounts of family meals, as long as they are low in salt. Make sure all nine common allergens have been introduced by now if they have not been already, and keep offering them regularly. If your baby has not tried a particular food yet, there is no rush, but variety now helps build a flexible eater.

Encouraging independence at nine months

Nine months is a good age to encourage independence at mealtimes. Let your baby hold their own spoon, even if most of the food ends up on the tray, and offer finger foods they can pick up themselves. Give them a cup with a little water and let them practise drinking, even if it spills. Sit with them at the table and eat the same food, so they can copy you. Praise their efforts and stay relaxed about the mess, because independence takes practice. Within a few weeks, most nine-month-olds become much more confident feeders, and mealtimes become easier for everyone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many meals should a nine-month-old eat?

Three meals a day, plus one or two snacks, alongside breast milk or formula. Milk is still an important part of the diet.

Can my nine-month-old eat finger foods at every meal?

Yes, as long as the foods are soft and safe. Finger foods at every meal help your baby practise chewing and self-feeding.

What if my baby still wants only purees?

Keep offering lumps and finger foods alongside purees. Most babies move on within a few weeks. If refusal continues, talk to your health visitor.

Important: every baby is different. Always follow your pediatrician's advice on when to start solids, which foods to offer, and how to introduce allergens. The guidance on this site is general information, not medical advice.
