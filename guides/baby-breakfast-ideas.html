Baby Breakfast Ideas

Breakfast is a great meal to practise eating, because babies are usually alert and hungry in the morning. A good baby breakfast combines iron, energy, and a little fruit or vegetable, and it does not need to take long to prepare. As your baby grows, breakfast changes from a spoonful of porridge to finger foods and family breakfasts. This guide gives simple breakfast ideas for each stage of weaning, from smooth first tastes to the chopped family foods of a one-year-old.

Breakfasts for six to eight months

At this stage, breakfast is usually a small bowl of porridge or baby rice made with breast milk or formula, served smooth and warm. Iron-fortified baby cereals are a useful choice because they add iron, which babies need from around six months. You can stir in a spoonful of fruit puree, such as apple or banana, for flavour. Mashed avocado on a soft toast finger, or a small amount of full-fat yoghurt with mashed fruit, are also good options. Keep portions small: a few spoonfuls is enough while milk is still the main food.

Breakfasts for eight to ten months

Babies this age can handle thicker textures and finger foods. Try scrambled egg strips, small pieces of omelette with spinach, or soft toast fingers with thinly spread nut butter. Porridge can be made thicker and topped with mashed berries or sliced banana. Yoghurt with soft fruit and a few pieces of soft pancake also works well. Offer water in a small cup alongside breakfast. This is a good age to start letting your baby pick up some of their own food, so include at least one finger food on the tray each morning.

Breakfasts for ten to twelve months

By now, your baby can eat much of what the family eats, chopped small. Try French toast fingers made with egg and milk, small pancakes with berries, porridge with chopped dried fruit, or toast with cream cheese and sliced cucumber. A small bowl of cereal with whole milk is fine at this age, as long as it is low in sugar and salt. Hard-boiled egg cut into strips, pieces of fruit, and yoghurt all make quick breakfasts. Keep sugar out of breakfast entirely: there is no need for jam, honey, or sweetened cereals.

Make-ahead breakfasts

Mornings are easier when breakfast is partly prepared. Overnight oats made with milk and grated apple keep in the fridge and can be served cold or warmed. Egg muffins made with vegetables keep for two days in the fridge and can be reheated quickly. Pancakes and French toast freeze well and can be defrosted overnight. Preparing a batch on Sunday saves time all week. Avoid ready-made baby breakfast pouches as an everyday habit: they are convenient, but they are often sweet, expensive, and do not teach your baby to handle different textures.

Making mornings easier

Mornings with a baby are busy, so prepare breakfast the night before whenever you can. Make overnight oats in a jar, cook a batch of egg muffins, or freeze pancakes in portions that can be toasted in the morning. Set out the highchair, bib, and bowl before bed, so the morning starts smoothly. Keep breakfast simple on weekdays and save the more involved recipes, such as French toast or homemade waffles, for the weekend. If your baby is not hungry first thing, offer a milk feed and try breakfast again after half an hour, because appetite varies and there is no need to force it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my baby have cow's milk at breakfast?

Cow's milk can be used in cooking and on cereal from six months, but as a main drink it should wait until twelve months, when it can replace formula.

What is the best first breakfast cereal?

An iron-fortified baby cereal or plain porridge oats made with milk are good first choices. Avoid cereals with added sugar or salt.

My baby refuses breakfast. Should I worry?

No. Some babies are not hungry first thing. Offer a milk feed and try breakfast again a little later, or offer a smaller amount.

Important: every baby is different. Always follow your pediatrician's advice on when to start solids, which foods to offer, and how to introduce allergens. The guidance on this site is general information, not medical advice.
